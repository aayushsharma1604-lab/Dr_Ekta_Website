## 3. PROCEDURES (procedure.html) — 12 detail entries

1. **Teeth Whitening** (image: teethwhitening.jpg)
   Discoloured teeth can lower your confidence. A very simple procedure gets you a
   lighter shade as desired. Two ways:
   - *Home bleach* — custom tray + bleaching kit, applied alternate days, ~4 times,
     desired result.
   - *Chairside bleach* — done faster in the dental chair, one or two sessions for a
     white, bright smile.

2. **Dental Veneers** (image: veneers.jpg)
   Chipped, discoloured and weakened teeth can be repaired while maintaining
   structural integrity. Two types:
   - *Ceramic/porcelain* — lab-fabricated, cemented; real looking teeth with desired
     shade, shape and lustre.
   - *Composite* — less time consuming, also gives desirable results.

3. **Smile Designing** (image: smile.jpg)
   Unsightly gaps, crooked teeth, broken teeth can be repaired easily; esthetic
   natural looking crowns given. Measurements taken, fabricated crowns placed on
   prepared teeth.

4. **Orthodontics — Braces & Removable Appliances** (image: ortho.jpg)
   Treatment for improper bites, crooked/malaligned teeth; improves appearance and
   function (occlusion).
   - *Fixed orthodontics (braces)* — orthodontic wires in stainless steel or ceramic
     braces; teeth aligned with respect to soft tissues; retainers maintain the
     improved position.
   - *Removable orthodontics* — plates, headgear, expansion appliances; functional and
     orthopaedic appliances in growing patients (age 5–14) to modify jaw dimensions,
     frequently followed by fixed multibracket therapy.

5. **Cosmetic Dentistry** (image: dent.jpg)
   Crooked, stained, chipped, fractured teeth transformed into natural, clean, shapely
   teeth within one or two sittings. Composite tooth coloured fillings for restoration
   of cavities, replacement of amalgam, worn/chipped teeth; used for contour, shape,
   colouring, relative position of front teeth.

6. **Dentures** (image: procedure_clip_image001.jpg)
   Prosthetic devices to replace missing teeth, supported by surrounding soft and hard
   tissues. Conventional dentures removable; some rely on bonding/clipping onto teeth
   or implants. Two categories by arch: **mandibular** or **maxillary**.
   Informal names: dental plate, false teeth, falsies.
   - **Removable partial dentures**
   - **Complete dentures (full)** — excellent fit even in difficult cases; hardly move
     while eating; reverse the "sunken" look; look extremely natural.

7. **Implants** (image: implant.jpg)
   Artificial tooth root replacement in prosthetic dentistry. Titanium anchor in a
   pre-drilled jaw socket supporting a crown, bridge or denture. Osseointegration with
   bone tissue creates stable support for new teeth.
   **Better than dentures and bridges:** improved oral hygiene; better tooth function;
   reduced bone loss; no need to reduce adjacent healthy tooth structure; looks natural
   and esthetic.

8. **Root Canal Treatment** (image: root.jpg)
   Main canals within the dentin of the tooth — part of the natural cavity consisting
   of the pulp chamber and canals. Infected/inflamed pulp (usually due to caries or
   fracture) is removed by endodontic therapy (root canal treatment).
   **How it is performed:** under local anaesthetic; antibiotics first if infected.
   Tooth opened through the crown, pulp removed, canals cleaned/enlarged with small
   files, filled and sealed. One or several visits; medication between visits if needed.
   Front/incisor/canine teeth: one canal; premolars: two; molars: three.
   Root-filled teeth are more brittle — a crown may be advised to protect remaining
   structure. Root canal treatment is usually very successful; can be repeated if
   infection returns.

9. **Crowns and Bridges** (image: crown.jpg)
   Tooth replaced to maintain alignment, chewing and speaking. Crown placement takes
   two visits: (1) decay removed, shaped, temporary crown placed; (2) lab-fabricated
   crown cemented. **Materials:** all ceramic; metal ceramic (silver/gold); metallic
   (silver/gold); zirconium. **Bridge** fills the gap where teeth are absent; fixed,
   removable only by a dental professional. **Why a bridge:** replace missing teeth,
   restore function/appearance, support lips and cheeks, prevent gum disease and
   speech disorders.

10. **Pediatric and Preventive Dentistry** (image: pedi.jpg)
    Age-defined specialty providing primary and comprehensive preventive and
    therapeutic oral health care for infants and children through adolescence,
    including those with special health care needs (hemophilia, congenital diseases,
    leukemias).
    - **First dental visit:** complete prenatal/perinatal/postnatal development
      reviewed; exam with child on parent's lap.
    - **Nursing bottle caries (BBTD):** bottle feeding past 12 months raises caries
      risk drastically. Guidelines: no bottle for sleep except water; cup before first
      birthday; wean from bottle at 12–14 months; supplementary solids at 4–6 months;
      juices only from a cup; oral hygiene from first primary tooth; first dental visit
      within six months of first tooth (no later than first birthday). Early
      demineralization treated with fluoride application and diet modification.
      Stainless steel/veneered crowns for decayed teeth; pulp therapy or extraction if
      decay reaches the pulp.

11. **Sedation and General Anaesthesia for Pediatric Patients**
    Needed when children suffer serious, potentially painful dental diseases which
    won't go away on their own; sedation or general anaesthesia may be recommended to
    deliver safe, comfortable treatment. Without treatment, dental diseases adversely
    affect learning, communication, nutrition and growth/development.

12. **Gum Diseases & Treatment (Gingivitis and Periodontitis)** (image: toothdiagram.gif)
    **Cause:** bacteria + mucus form sticky colorless "plaque"; hardens into "tartar";
    only professional cleaning removes it.
    - **Gingivitis:** red, swollen, bleeding gums; mild form; reversible with daily
      brushing/flossing and regular cleaning.
    - **Periodontitis:** gums pull away forming infected "pockets"; toxins break down
      bone/connective tissue; teeth loosen; must be removed if untreated.
    **Risk factors:** smoking; hormonal changes; diabetes; genetic susceptibility;
    medications; stress.
    **Symptoms (advanced):** bad breath; red/swollen gums; tender or bleeding gums;
    painful chewing; loose teeth; sensitive teeth.
    **Treatment:** control infection; scaling (scraping tartar above/below gumline) and
    root planing (removing rough root spots); quit tobacco for better outcomes.

## 6. FAQ (faq.html) — 9 Q&A

**Q. What is tooth erosion?** — Tooth enamel is a hard, mineralised tissue covering
the teeth. Its mineralisation is affected by excessive acid production — due to
decreased saliva, acid reflux disease, eating disorder bulimia, or acid-containing
juices and food. *Signs:* discoloration/yellowing; sensitivity to hot and cold foods;
rounded margins from enamel wear; sandblasted or transparent look.

**Q. What is a tooth abscess?** — An infection; pus and swelling of the soft gum
tissues around the tooth. Develops from decay or trauma (broken tooth). Bacteria
enter through a cavity and infect the pulp. Infection can spread through the mouth
and body; a root canal is usually the only way to save an abscessed tooth. See the
dentist right away if you suspect one.

**Q. What is tooth sensitivity?** — Pain or discomfort from sweets, cold air, hot
drinks, cold drinks or ice cream; sometimes from brushing/flossing. Sensitive teeth
can be treated.

**Q. What is a veneer?** — A thin shell of porcelain or composite custom-made and
cemented to the front of the tooth; treats slightly crooked, discoloured, chipped
teeth and closes spaces.

**Q. What is bruxism?** — Clenching/grinding of teeth, especially at night; causes
pressure on muscles, tissues and jaw; jaw disorders, pain, soreness, headaches,
earaches, damaged teeth, TMJ problems. A clear night guard can be made to protect
against clenching/grinding.

**Q. What is an implant?** — A metal (usually titanium) device placed surgically
into the jawbone to replace missing teeth; permanent, anchors an artificial tooth
such as a crown, bridge or denture.

**Q. Difference between white and silver fillings?** — Silver (amalgam) fillings:
50% mercury + 50% other metals. White (composite): acrylic + glass particles.
Differences also in cost, strength and appearance.

**Q. What is periodontitis?** — Serious, advanced gum disease with bone loss.
Irreversible. Gums/bone supporting teeth become damaged; teeth loosen and fall out.
Occurs when gingivitis is left untreated. Linked to increased risk of stroke and
heart attack, higher blood sugar, and possibly premature/low-weight births. Prevention:
brush twice daily, floss daily, regular dental checkups.

**Q. What is a root canal treatment?** — A procedure fixing a tooth by removing the
pulp chamber and filling it with suitable material; usually performed when the decay
has reached the nerve or the tooth is infected.

## 7. ARTICLES & PUBLICATIONS (article.html)

### Article 1: "Implants! A permanent solution to tooth loss"
(image: implants-a-permanent-solution-to-tooth-loss.jpg)
- Fear of dentures/bridges: stability problems, unrealistic expectations, meticulous
  hygiene needed for bridges.
- **Who needs implants:** grossly decayed teeth requiring removal or periodontitis
  patients; gums and bone must be disease-free first.
- **Not everyone is a candidate:** tobacco smokers, diabetics, osteoporotic bone may
  be unsuitable; blood tests + OPG/dentascan x-rays determine bone; bone grafts need
  6–12 months healing before implant placement; exact length/thickness determined.
- **Procedure:** local anaesthesia (or sedation/anaesthesia); gum incision, drilling
  hole, implant placed snugly, sutures removed after one week; bone fuses in 3–6
  months (temporary bridge possible); second sitting places crown.
- **Failure causes:** unhealthy bone; smoking; infection; surgical trauma — but
  failure is minimal if done properly and post-op instructions followed; failed
  implants can be replaced.

### Article 2: "Teeth whitening, a simple way to a beautiful smile."
(image: teeth-whitening-a-simple-way-to-a.jpg)
- Scaling and polishing first; two bleaching routes: take-home kit (custom lab tray,
  gel per manufacturer direction, results over time) vs chairside bleaching (1–2
  hours; more concentrated peroxide; gum-line gel + laser to set; bleaching gel set
  by UV radiation).
- **Does bleaching harm?** Precautions for gingival recession/thin enamel
  (sensitivity); sensitivity usually subsides after 48 hours; avoid hot/cold foods
  for a day or two; not for children or pregnant/breastfeeding women.
- **Whitening systems:** BriteSmile, Zoom!, Opalescence, Pola Office — dentist chooses.
- **Cost:** depends on dentist credentials, clinic, locality, whitening system.
- **How long it lasts:** 2–3 years; depends on eating/smoking/drinking/brushing
  habits; can be repeated.
